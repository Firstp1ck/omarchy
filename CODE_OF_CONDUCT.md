# Code of conduct, regrettably

> **Parody notice:** This is an unofficial, sarcastic document in a fork. It is not the policy of upstream Omarchy, Basecamp, 37signals, DHH, or anyone else who has had the good sense not to approve it.

## Our pledge

Upstream Omarchy perfected the smallest possible code of conduct: no file at all. This fork has recklessly replaced that zero-byte masterpiece with several hundred words.

We welcome people who like computers. No political loyalty oath, personality test, ideological checksum, or proof of owning the correct podcast subscriptions is required.

We also expect basic decency. This is apparently controversial enough to require headings.

## Our standards

Acceptable behavior includes:

- Show up, do useful work, and let the patch make the argument.
- Disagree directly. Criticize code, decisions, and ideas instead of inventing a psychiatric profile for their authors.
- Help newcomers without demanding that they first write a compiler, rebuild Arch from source, or pass an oral examination in a public chat.
- Respect privacy. A bug report rarely improves after publishing somebody's home address.
- Keep technical spaces technical. Nobody needs a geopolitical loyalty declaration before reviewing a shell script.
- Remember that stars are not ballots, reactions are not case law, and the issue tracker is not parliament.
- Use a fork when the maintainers choose a direction you cannot tolerate. Git shipped the original appeals process years ago.

Unacceptable behavior includes:

- Threats, stalking, doxxing, slurs, discriminatory attacks, sexual harassment, or sustained personal abuse.
- Pile-ons, brigading, and summoning an audience to punish a named person.
- Defamatory allegations disguised as community feedback.
- Turning a technical disagreement into a referendum on whether somebody deserves to exist on the internet.
- Demanding commercial customer service from volunteers, then asking to speak to the manager of the gift.
- Treating maintainer authority as a divine right to be needlessly cruel. A benevolent dictator who forgets the first word is just a dictator with repository permissions.

## Governance without the ceremonial robes

This project is not a democracy. It is also not a hereditary monarchy, despite the occasional crown emoji.

People who do the work will make most project decisions. Productive effort earns influence. It does not earn a license to threaten, stalk, discriminate against, or harass other people. Those are different concepts, even if putting them in one paragraph ruins a perfectly good culture war.

Maintainers may close off-topic threads, hide abusive comments, reject contributions, or block repeat offenders. No Ministry of Vibes, tribunal of internet elders, or 48-page jurisprudence manual is required. Moderation should address observable behavior, not presumed politics or identity.

If new facts show that a moderation decision was wrong, maintainers may reconsider it. This practice is known in some circles as changing your mind.

## Scope

These expectations apply to issues, pull requests, discussions, and other spaces controlled by this fork. They do not claim authority over upstream Omarchy or unrelated community spaces.

Use GitHub's reporting and blocking tools for platform abuse. This parody creates no private reporting channel and should not be mistaken for a secret enforcement process.

## The source material we have irresponsibly turned into policy

The parody above riffs on public statements. The quotations below are real; the jokes elsewhere are not quotations.

- When asked to add a code of conduct, DHH wrote: ["We're going to have exactly none of this nonsense in Omarchy."](https://github.com/basecamp/omarchy/issues/1913#issuecomment-3337195238) We have honored this instruction by writing exactly none of it across several sections.
- When someone proposed a formal "No Code of Conduct," upstream collaborator Ryan Hughes replied: ["Literally having no code of conduct is the original no code of conduct."](https://github.com/basecamp/omarchy/issues/1969#issuecomment-3338563881) This file is therefore an unauthorized sequel.
- DHH has argued that ["productive effort is the correct moral basis of power"](https://world.hey.com/dhh/open-source-is-neither-a-community-nor-a-democracy-606abdab) in open source. Here, that means good contributions earn influence. They still do not unlock the premium harassment package.
- DHH has also asked people not to express technical enthusiasm by ["defacing collaboration organs or slandering those who JavaScript differently."](https://world.hey.com/dhh/open-source-hooliganism-and-the-typescript-meltdown-a474bfda) Against all odds, that is solid conduct advice.
